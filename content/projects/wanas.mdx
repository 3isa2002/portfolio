## Problem

Voice assistants speak formal Arabic at best, and they are a speaker, not a companion. I wanted a
small robot on the desk that you talk to in Egyptian Arabic, freely and without pressing a button,
that answers in a real human-sounding voice with feeling, with a face that moves as it talks, and
that remembers you.

## Approach

Wanas is three parts: an ESP32-S3 robot, a FastAPI "brain" in the cloud, and a React admin
dashboard.

- **Conversation without a button.** Touch it once and talk. The robot learns the room's noise
  level, detects when you start and when you stop (0.7 s of quiet), and listens again after it
  answers. Your voice streams to the server while you are still speaking.
- **One call to hear and answer.** The server sends the audio together with your memories to
  Gemini Flash-Lite, which transcribes, replies in Egyptian Arabic and picks the emotion and face
  in a single request. Live questions (weather, news, matches) go to a second Gemini model with
  Google Search.
- **A human voice with feeling.** Gemini writes voice cues into its reply (laughing, a sigh, a
  softer voice); Fish Audio performs them in a trained Egyptian voice and streams the audio back,
  so the reply starts about 2 seconds after you stop talking. edge-tts takes over if Fish Audio is
  down.
- **A living face.** Eyes and mouth animate at 30 fps on a round 240×240 screen; the mouth follows
  the loudness of the outgoing audio, and the expression changes at the moment the emotion lands
  in the speech.
- **Firmware that never blocks.** Five FreeRTOS tasks split over both cores (face, app, audio in,
  audio out, network) and talk only through queues, a ring buffer and atomics, so the face never
  waits on the network.
- **Memory and safety.** It remembers facts about you and reminders in SQLite. Every robot has its
  own key, Wi-Fi and secrets are entered on a setup page and stored on the device (none in the
  code), the link is encrypted (WSS with certificate checks) and usage is rate-limited.

The hardware: an ESP32-S3 (16 MB flash, 8 MB PSRAM), a GC9A01 round display on SPI, an INMP441
microphone and a MAX98357A amplifier on I2S, a TTP223 touch sensor, and a LiPo battery with a
TP4056 charger, all documented in a full wiring map and bill of materials. The enclosure is a
parametric three-part OpenSCAD model, exported to STL and checked for printing.

## Result

A working pilot on a breadboard: the server, firmware v2 and the admin dashboard (users, chat
history, usage) run end to end, and the enclosure is ready to print. Next: a "يا وَنَس" wake word
instead of the touch, reminders spoken at their time, cloud deployment (Docker + Caddy on Google
Cloud) and battery power inside the enclosure.
