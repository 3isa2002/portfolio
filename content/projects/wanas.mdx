## Problem

I wanted a desk companion that understands plain speech instead of fixed commands, and answers with
sound and a face on a small screen. The catch: it had to run on a microcontroller, inside its memory
and timing limits, with the language understanding happening in the cloud.

## Approach

Voice and touch come in on an ESP32-S3. The request goes over Wi-Fi to the Gemini API (HTTPS/REST),
which detects the intent of the natural-language command; each detected intent maps to a device
response or action.

The hardware is four peripherals around the ESP32-S3: a GC9A01 round display on SPI, an INMP441
MEMS microphone and a MAX98357A Class-D amplifier on two separate I2S ports, and a TTP223 touch
sensor. I calculated the 3.3 V / 5 V power budget, added capacitors to stop Wi-Fi brownout resets,
and documented the wiring map.

## Result

A working desk device that responds to natural-language commands with sound and an animated face,
handling sensor input, Wi-Fi communication and response latency within the board's memory and
timing constraints.
