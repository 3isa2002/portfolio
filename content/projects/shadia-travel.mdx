## Problem

Shadia sells organized trips inside Egypt, run by tour companies, by the seat on a bus. Customers
needed to find a trip, pick a departure date, a pickup point and a seat, and follow their booking
from a phone or a browser. The team needed one place to run all of it: trips and their dates,
bookings and payments, the tour companies, a loyalty program with partner shops, support, and
numbers that show what is selling.

## Approach

I gathered the requirements with the client and built the platform end to end as one monorepo:
three applications on a single Supabase backend. Every price, seat and booking rule lives in the
database, so the app and the website can never disagree.

### The mobile app

A Flutter app (Riverpod, go_router) in Arabic, English and Franco (Arabic in Latin letters), also published as a web
build at [mobile.shadia.app](https://mobile.shadia.app). It replaced an earlier React Native
version.

- Browse and search trips with filters, open a trip with its photos, day-by-day program, departure
  dates and pickup points, choose seats on the bus map and book.
- Sign-in by phone number with an SMS code, sent through a Supabase hook to an Egyptian SMS
  provider.
- Bookings with QR tickets, a wallet, a loyalty program whose points turn into vouchers at partner
  cafés and restaurants, and support tickets.

### The customer website

[shadia.app](https://www.shadia.app) is a Next.js site that follows the app screen by screen: the
same steps, texts, rules and booking statuses, checked against a written parity checklist. Its
translations are generated from the app's own dictionary.

- It books through the same database functions as the app, so a seat is held for one customer
  only and the total is always computed on the server, never in the browser.
- Online payment through Paymob is built in: the server creates the payment from the booking's own
  amount, the card is entered on Paymob's page, and a payment counts only when Paymob's signed
  webhook confirms it. It sits behind a switch until the gateway contract is active; until then
  bookings are paid in cash or by InstaPay and confirmed by staff.
- Booking emails with an Arabic PDF ticket, server-rendered pages for speed and search engines,
  and a content security policy.

### The staff portal

The admin portal is where the business runs. It is a Next.js dashboard, and what a person sees in
it depends on their role:

- **Trips.** Create and edit trips in three languages with a photo gallery, pickup points, bus
  types with their seat maps, and recurring departure dates, each with its own price and seats.
- **Bookings.** Confirm, reject or cancel bookings, record payments, and see who sits where.
- **Tour companies.** Each company's staff sign in to see only their own trips. Their changes
  arrive as edit requests that an admin reviews as a diff and approves or rejects.
- **Loyalty and partners.** Sponsors, vouchers and a points ledger. A partner's cashier signs in
  to a separate redeem screen and scans the customer's QR voucher.
- **Customers and support.** Customer accounts and wallets, and support tickets with replies.
- **Staff and permissions.** 27 permissions in 8 groups (trips, bookings, companies, partners,
  staff, support, customers, analytics), with the sensitive ones marked, assigned per employee.
- **Analytics.** A separate analytics area with 16 reports: live activity, screen flow, search
  log, demand, the booking funnel, trip performance, revenue, customer segments, retention and
  campaign audiences, with CSV export. An AI analyst sends the aggregated numbers (no names or
  phone numbers) to Claude and gets back a performance report and a plan for the week, in Arabic.

### The backend

Supabase Postgres with row-level security. The schema is more than 60 migrations; the ones that guard
bookings, access and account deletion have tests that run on an in-memory Postgres. CI runs the
website's lint, type checks, unit tests and build, the portal's build and the migration tests on
every pull request, and Vercel deploys the main branch.

## Result

The website, the app's web build and the staff portal are live for the client on shadia.app, with
50 trips on sale. The repo holds 460 commits since July 2026, including a full rebuild of the
website onto the app's booking rules and a security pass over the shared backend.
